Mapped against BenchJack's eight published flaw classes, **Assay detects four of eight**, and two of those (V1, V7) on one ecosystem only. |
| Slice 31a | The gaps, named rather than softened. | `docs/COVERAGE.md` | **V2** (answers shipped with the test) — nothing enumerates the runtime filesystem for a reference solution, and the public-URL variant is not even executable by an auditor whose own sandbox runs with `network=False`. **V3** (RCE into the evaluator) — no static analysis of verifier source, no `DefectClass`; a Challenger might land one and it would surface as `REWARD_HACKABLE` with no evidence field ever saying RCE. **V8** (excessive permissions) — the clearest gap: Assay *obeys* a task's declared `network_mode` and `environment_mode` and never asserts on them. **V4** (LLM-judge injection) — excluded by an architectural rule rather than a missing feature, so the honest form is that Assay declines a judge-scored environment rather than falsely clearing it. |
| Slice 31b | The map in the other direction, because a one-directional one is always flattering. | 14 `DefectClass` members | **Nine of fourteen have no BenchJack counterpart**, for three distinct reasons and none of them a deficiency in the taxonomy. **Direction:** V1–V8 are all false-*positive* mechanisms, while `SPEC_VERIFIER_MISMATCH` is false-*negative* — a benchmark too harsh to pass is not "jackable". **Layer:** contamination and shortcut leakage are dataset properties that corrupt the number whether or not any agent misbehaves. **Kind of statement:** trivial-floor, separability, determinism and difficulty are statistical properties of the reward surface, not mechanisms — and a taxonomy derived from *reported incidents* has no entry for "this environment's scores are uninformative", because nobody files an incident about that. |
| Slice 31c | The distinction that decides what is worth building. | `scored_entries`, capability gating | **"No probe exists" and "a probe exists but no adapter can feed it" are different failures, and only the second is fixed by writing adapters.** V1/V7 outside Harbor, V5 on Harbor, and `SHORTCUT_LEAK`/`CONTAMINATION_*` on nearly every real ecosystem are all the second kind. So is the corpus's one remaining miss: `inspect_evals/boolq` carries `SHORTCUT_LEAK`, `WildInspectAdapter` supplies no train split, and the probe declines before it runs. Left open deliberately — inventing a train split for somebody else's benchmark would close it by constructing the structure being audited. |
| Slice 31d | The test that keeps it complete. | `tests/test_published_claims.py` | Asserts every one of the 14 `DefectClass` members and all eight V-classes appear, and that the document's closing example is still the environment actually missed. **It failed on its first run**, catching three classes the hand-written matrix had silently dropped — `KNOWN_WRONG_PASSES`, `DIFFICULTY_SATURATED`, `DIFFICULTY_IMPOSSIBLE`. An omission in a coverage document reads as coverage, which is the failure mode worth a test. |
