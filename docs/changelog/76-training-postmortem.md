| Slice 25a | **New finding — train and eval are different scaffolds.** The playbook's third law is that a different agent scaffold is a different task. | `src/assay/challenger/grpo.py` SYSTEM (614 chars) vs `src/assay/challenger/prompted.py` SYSTEM (1462 chars) | **A single-shot policy was trained and a ten-turn agent is measured.** The trained prompt says *"You get ONE shot: emit the entire policy at once"*. The probe evaluates with `PromptedChallenger`, an interactive loop that accumulates history across ten turns and is explicitly told to do reconnaissance before acting. Whatever the training run had learned would have been evaluated under a scaffold it never saw. This was not recorded anywhere in the original write-up. |
| Slice 25b | Whether the run could have produced a gradient at all. | run.json: 9 distinct completions across 2400 rollouts, 1918 of them one 60-character string | **No SFT step.** Base model straight to GRPO. Group-relative advantage is `(r − mean)/std` within a prompt's rollouts; with 85.7% of groups byte-identical there is nothing to compute an advantage over. That is not slow learning, and it is not a GRPO problem — it is a model that has never seen the task. The playbook's fourth law: SFT teaches the workflow, RL amplifies what already sometimes works. Gold trajectories to rejection-sample already exist in `results/trajectories/` and `results/challenger_ablation*.json`. |
| Slice 25c | Whether the training mix could pay. | `scripts/reward_landscape.py`, pre-registered before the GPU | **Half of it could not, and this was known in advance.** 39 prompts, 19 with any signal, **51.3% flat**. An environment where every candidate scores the same contributes exactly zero gradient however many steps it gets. Running the landscape before buying hardware was exactly right — the miss was training on the half it had just measured as dead. The fifth law asks for a 10–80% solve band; this was measured and then not applied. |
| Slice 25d | Whether the reward has a degenerate optimum. | `results/reward_landscape.json` rows with `"best_policy": "empty"` | **Yes, and the original write-up called it correctly.** `{"actions": []}` scores 1.0 wherever a verifier rubber-stamps, ~0.0 elsewhere, and is never negative — so it is a true global optimum of this reward on this corpus. That is a true reading of the reward rather than a bug in it, as recorded. For *training* it is still fatal: the cheapest hack is the optimum and it transfers to nothing. `harbor/vacuous-tests` and `fixture/solved_at_reset` belong in the eval set, not the mix. |
| Slice 25e | Whether to try again, given evidence that did not exist when the run was designed. | today's k=4 harbor runs; `results/full_run_llm.json` | **No — and the reason is new.** The composite arm driven by a frontier model over ten turns **matches the scripted floor and does not beat it**, four runs of four. The `qwen3:8b` baselines score 2415.0 and 2497.0 against `stratified_random`'s 1587.0. If a frontier model in an interactive loop cannot beat a four-policy fixed repertoire, a LoRA'd 1.7B will not. The bottleneck is a corpus that is 22/24 self-authored, not an attacker that already matches its own floor. Recorded as a decision with its evidence rather than a plan quietly dropped. |
| Slice 25f | The one eval fix worth making regardless of whether training resumes. | `src/assay/challenger/prompted.py`, `scripts/challenger_calibration.py`, `results/calibration.json` | **The calibration instrument was measuring almost nothing, and said so itself.** `instrument_limit` in the results file: *"the attacker emits a boolean, so only 2 distinct forecast values appear"* — a reliability diagram with two bins, and an ECE that reduces to the gap between two points. `solves_the_task` is now a probability in [0, 1] with the prompt asking for the range explicitly. Traces recorded before the change carry bools and are read at the endpoints, so the existing corpus scores identically (69 pairs, mean Brier 0.086957) rather than being silently rebinned — and `instrument_limit` still fires on them, correctly, because those traces genuinely cannot support more resolution. A float is truthy at 0.1, so every consumer thresholds at `SOLVED_AT` instead of testing truthiness; that trap is pinned as a test. |
